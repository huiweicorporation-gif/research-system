CLAIMS TO VERIFY (from the meeting's memory of the papers; NOT yet checked against the primary texts. Check each
before writing it; say a disputed one as a hypothesis or cut it)
1. Body lice (living in clothing) split from head lice; the split dates clothing.
   - Kittler, Kayser & Stoneking 2003, Current Biology: an early estimate around 72,000 +/- 42,000 years.
   - Toups, Kitchen, Light & Reed 2011, Molecular Biology and Evolution: a later estimate of roughly
     83,000-170,000 years.
   Say it with its range and that it dates clothing, not fur loss. Head lice and body lice are usually treated as
   two forms (ecotypes) of ONE species, Pediculus humanus: never say "two species".
2. Fur loss happened much earlier, dated by another clock: Rogers, Iltis & Wooding 2004, Current Anthropology,
   the MC1R gene suggests dark skin under the lost fur by about 1.2 million years ago. MC1R is a pigment gene:
   use it only for the fur-loss date; say nothing about skin colour between groups of people.
   The two dates do not prove each other: say so.
3. Why the fur went (competing hypotheses, none proven):
   - Fewer parasites: Pagel & Bodmer 2003, Proceedings of the Royal Society B ("a naked ape would have fewer
     parasites").
   - Cooling while active in the heat: Wheeler's thermoregulation work (1980s-1990s); Ruxton & Wilkinson 2011,
     PNAS, challenge or refine the heat argument: check which way.
   Present them side by side; the parasite idea leads because it is the lice episode.
4. Lice as "living fossils" of human history: Reed et al. 2004, PLoS Biology (head-louse lineages and archaic
   humans). Use only if verified; keep it to one scene.
Do NOT use: pubic lice (their gorilla origin is a real paper, but the topic is off the channel), any sexual angle,
any comparison of hair or skin between groups of people.
