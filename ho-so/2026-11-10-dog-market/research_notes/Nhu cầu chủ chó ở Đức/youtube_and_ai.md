# German dog YouTube, and how German audiences react to AI-made / stock-footage videos (2024–2026)

Research date: 2026-11-10. About 20 tool calls. Several sources are secondary (aggregators, stats sites); each is flagged where it matters.

## 1. Who are the big German dog YouTube channels, and what formats do they use?

### Takeaway
The German dog-training YouTube scene that I could verify is led by **real, certified trainers on camera with real dogs** (Doguniversity, Steffis, DogsTV/Ines Wille, Stadtfelle, Steve Kaye, mydog365), all in the 0.03–0.43 M subscriber range. I could not verify subscriber numbers for Martin Rütter, Hundeo or vet channels. I found no faceless dog channel among the leaders.

### Cited Findings
- Doguniversity – Hundetraining mit Daniel: about 428,000 subscribers on the YouTube page (a third-party profile says 424,000); Daniel is a trained, certified dog trainer who appears on camera — [YouTube](https://m.youtube.com/c/DogUniversityHundetrainingmitDaniel)
- Steffis – Online-Hundetraining (Hundeerziehung Hundepension Salostowitz): about 211,000 subscribers per a third-party stats site — [youtubers.me](https://us.youtubers.me/hundeerziehung-hundepension-salostowitz/youtuber-stats)
- DogsTV Hundewelt (Ines Wille): the channel's own site claims 200,000 YouTube subscribers. Its topics are breeds, Qualzucht (breeding for harmful traits) and general dog knowledge, so it is an educational and explainer channel — [dogstv.de](https://www.dogstv.de/)
- Stadtfelle DogTraining (Dirk & team): about 101,000 subscribers — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@StadtFelle)
- Steve Kaye: the sources disagree. His website says "over 19,000" subscribers (an old page), while the YouTube metadata says about 112,000. The website addresses viewers with "Dein Weg zum Traum-Hundeteam" ("your way to the dream dog team"), using the informal "du" form — [steve-kaye.de](https://steve-kaye.de/); [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCZ8P5rosutm5WV2OtgQNxUQ)
- mydog365 calls itself "Deutschlands größte Online-Hundeschule" (Germany's biggest online dog school), but it has only about 29,700 YouTube subscribers. The claim is the channel's own and is not independently checked — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/c/mydog365)
- A Tubesights "Top 10 Hunde Kanäle" ranking exists, but the page could not be fetched (its TLS certificate has expired) — [tubesights.de](https://tubesights.de/top-10-hunde-kanaele-youtube-ranking/)
- Martin Rütter is described as Germany's best-known dog expert. He founded his first dog school in 1995 with the DOGS concept ("Dog Orientated Guiding System"), had his own VOX TV docu-series "Der Hundeprofi" from 2008, and has drawn more than 2.5 M visitors to his live shows. His reach is TV, books and touring, and I could not establish it as a YouTube channel — [leinetal24](https://www.leinetal24.de/boulevard/das-wussten-sie-noch-nicht-ueber-hundeprofi-martin-ruetter-zr-94488924.html); [infranken](https://www.infranken.de/lk/bamberg/martin-ruetter-kommt-fuer-drei-shows-nach-oberfranken-und-raeumt-vorab-mit-falschen-vorurteilen-auf-art-5835260,PRINT)

### Inferences
- The proof of authority in this niche is a trainer's face, credentials and the trainer working with real dogs. A faceless channel cannot copy that, so it has to set itself apart on something else: sourced science or explainer value (like DogsTV's knowledge and breed topics), not "training advice from a trainer".
- The informal "du" form looks like the norm for trainer brands (Steve Kaye, the channel names). This is an inference from a small sample.
- A mid-size dog channel in Germany sits around 100–450 k subscribers. The DACH dog niche is far smaller than the English one.

## 2. Faceless or AI-generated German dog and pet channels: examples and how they are received

### Takeaway
I found **no press coverage of a specific German AI dog channel**, and **no trace of "Gerd Hallmann"** anywhere on the web. German tech media do cover "KI-Slop" (AI slop) heavily: Germany is in the global top group for AI-slop views, and YouTube deleted large AI channels in 2026. Viewers increasingly ask "real or AI?" about animal videos.

### Cited Findings
- A search for "Gerd Hallmann" together with YouTube or KI (AI) returned no matching results; the only near match was the futurist Gerd Leonhard, who is unrelated. A grep of the local workspace for "Hallmann" timed out without finishing — [search results, e.g. futuristgerd.com](https://futuristgerd.com/de/tag/digital-ethics/)
- Kapwing study (November 2025), as reported by German outlets: 21 % of the first 500 Shorts shown to a new account (104 videos) were AI-generated, and 33 % (165) were "brainrot". 278 pure slop channels together have more than 63 billion views and an estimated income of about 117 M USD a year — [WinFuture](https://winfuture.de/news,155873.html); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.com/YouTube-versinkt-im-AI-Slop-Ein-Drittel-der-Shorts-fuer-Neulinge-ist-KI-Muell-oder-Brainrot.1192993.0.html); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/one-in-five-youtube-shorts-shown-to-new-users-is-ai-generated-slop-study-finds/)
- AI-slop channels in the German market have about 1.23 billion views. That is behind South Korea and Spain, but still in the global top group — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.com/YouTube-versinkt-im-AI-Slop-Ein-Drittel-der-Shorts-fuer-Neulinge-ist-KI-Muell-oder-Brainrot.1192993.0.html); [GameStar](https://www.gamestar.de/artikel/youtube-ki-inhalte-analyse,3445696.html)
- YouTube deleted 16 large AI channels with more than 4.7 billion views and about 35 M subscribers in total (July 2026). The report stresses that the target was mass production with no original idea or fully automated content, not AI use in itself. This is a secondary source — [Samsung Magazine DE](https://samsungmagazine.eu/de/2026/07/29/youtube-nekompromisne-cisti-platformu-47-miliardy-zhlednuti-je-pryc-kvuli-ai-spamu/)
- YouTube CEO Neal Mohan called fighting low-quality AI content a top priority for 2026 — [Meedia](https://meedia.de/news/beitrag/21045-so-wollen-youtube-amp-co-gegen-ai-slop-vorgehen.html)
- A German gutefrage.net thread (February 2026) about a dog music video asks "real dogs or AI?"; one user says pictures and videos can hardly be trusted any more — [gutefrage.net](https://www.gutefrage.net/diskussion/echte-hunde-oder-ki-was-meint-ihr)
- A German YouTube video contrasts AI-generated dog images with real dog photos and questions how much the "likes" on AI dog images are worth — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7sBGu3BQ2kA)

### Inferences
- German viewers are primed to spot and question AI animal content. Using **real footage of real dogs** (Pexels video rather than AI-generated dogs) is a clear advantage, because the "is this dog real?" objection does not arise.
- The main thing that gets a channel rejected, both by YouTube and by viewers, is AI-generated imagery plus a mass-produced feel. AI narration over real footage is a smaller risk, but a robotic voice is still a common complaint (see question 3).

### Gaps
- The "Gerd Hallmann" example could not be verified. Treat it as unconfirmed unless the caller has a direct link, for example in the project's own radar data.
- I found no heise, t3n or Spiegel article about a specific German AI pet channel.
- I found no German-language data on comment sentiment towards AI voices in pet videos.

## 3. German public attitude to AI-generated media (trust, labelling, AI voices)

### Takeaway
German-speaking audiences are clearly sceptical. A majority are uncomfortable with mainly AI-made news, and 83–85 % want AI content labelled, yet only about a quarter feel able to recognise it. Acceptance rises sharply when AI only *assists* a human who stays responsible.

### Cited Findings
- Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2025, German part (Leibniz-HBI, YouGov, about 2,000 respondents per country): 54 % of German internet users are rather or very uncomfortable with news made mainly by AI with some human oversight, 26 % are undecided and 13 % have no concerns. 34 % are comfortable when news is made mainly by journalists with some AI help — [ZDF Datenblick, 3 November 2025](https://www.zdf.de/unternehmen/organisation/publikationen/datenblick-akzeptanz-ki-mensch-maschine-100.html); [Leibniz-HBI](https://leibniz-hbi.de/en/hbi-news/pressinfo/german-findings-of-the-reuters-institute-digital-news-report-2025/)
- Marketagent "AI Slop" survey (May 2026, n = 1,016): 85 % call for clear labelling of AI content, and only about a quarter feel confident they can recognise it. Marketagent is an Austrian institute and the PDF text could not be extracted, so whether the sample is AT or DE is **unverified**. Figures come from the search snippet — [Marketagent PDF](https://b2b.marketagent.com/media/kb1pdg3c/pressecharts_ai-slop_mai-2026.pdf)
- RTR (Austrian regulator), 2025 study on AI and trust in media: more than 70 % rate AI use negatively, 85 % want AI content always checked by humans, and 83 % want clear labelling — [RTR PDF](https://www.rtr.at/medien/aktuelles/publikationen/Publikationen/Publikationen_2025/Studie_KI_Medienvertrauen2025.pdf)
- Ipsos KI-Monitor 2026 (32 countries): 52 % of Germans would trust content or answers less if they were AI-generated, and 29 % say it would make no difference (an advertising context) — [Ipsos DE](https://www.ipsos.com/de-de/node/1080686); [onlinemarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/technologie/ki-werbung-schwindet-vertrauen)
- Grabarz & Partner / Macromedia study: an AI label on *text* hurts trust more, while the effect for *images* is smaller and can even be positive for brand trust — [onlinemarketing.de summary via search](https://onlinemarketing.de/?p=386920)

### Inferences
- The framing that works for a faceless channel is **"human-researched, AI-assisted"**: sourced facts, a named editorial owner, and AI used openly as a tool. This matches the 34 % versus 13 % gap in comfort between AI-assisted and AI-led content.
- Labelling should be offered proactively (a line in the description or a pinned comment), because demand for labels is about 85 %.

### Gaps
- I found no Bitkom survey specifically on labelling AI content or trust in it; the Bitkom results I found concern AI adoption in companies.
- I found no German survey specifically on the **acceptance of synthetic or AI voices** (as narrators, in audiobooks or on YouTube).
- I did not reach the Bertelsmann Stiftung's AI-attitude work.

## 4. EU AI Act Article 50: labelling duties, dates, Digital Omnibus, and YouTube's disclosure

### Takeaway
Article 50 has applied since **2 August 2026**. Deployers must disclose **deepfakes**, meaning realistic AI image, audio or video that resembles real persons, objects, places or events and would falsely look authentic. They must also disclose AI-generated **text on matters of public interest**, unless it went through human review or editorial control. The Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026) did **not** delay the deployer labelling duties. It only gave providers of systems already on the market until 2 December 2026 for machine-readable marking. YouTube's own disclosure rule is separate but overlaps.

### Cited Findings
- Art. 50(2): providers of generative AI systems must mark outputs in a machine-readable way so they are detectable as AI-generated. Tools that only assist standard editing are excluded — [AI Act Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Art. 50(4): deployers of deepfakes "shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated". For evidently artistic, creative, satirical or fictional works, the duty is limited to disclosing the AI content in a way that does not spoil the work. AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest must be disclosed, unless it has had human review or editorial control and a person holds editorial responsibility — [AI Act Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Art. 3(60) defines a deepfake as AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear authentic — [AI Act Art. 50 page](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Art. 50(5): the information must be "clear and distinguishable" at the latest at first exposure. Application date: 2 August 2026 (Art. 113) — [AI Act Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Digital Omnibus on AI: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026. It moved the Annex III high-risk duties from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027. The only Article 50 relief is narrow: providers of generative systems on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 for 50(2) marking. The deployer-side deepfake labelling had no grace period. These are secondary legal commentaries, and the Official Journal text was not checked — [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [CSA research note](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-20260729/); [Technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/13/eu-ai-act-deadlines-after-the-digital-omnibus-what-applies-from-december-2026-to-august-2028/)
- YouTube "altered or synthetic content" disclosure: creators must disclose realistic content that is AI-generated or meaningfully altered, for example a real person shown saying or doing something they did not, altered footage of real events or places, or a realistic scene that never happened. No disclosure is needed for clearly unrealistic content, minor aesthetic edits, AI help with scripts, titles, thumbnails or infographics, captions, audio cleanup, or **cloning your own voice**. The page does not mention the EU AI Act. Creators who keep failing to disclose may get a label applied by YouTube, or penalties up to removal or suspension from YPP. Disclosure itself does not reduce reach or monetisation — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)

### Inferences
- **Real Pexels footage plus an AI voice reading sourced facts is not a deepfake** under Art. 3(60). The footage is real, and a generic AI narrator does not imitate a real person. The YouTube label is most likely not required for such a video. It *would* be required for AI-generated realistic dogs or scenes, or an AI voice that imitates a real person.
- The "public interest text" clause is unlikely to fit dog-behaviour explainers, and human editorial review exempts it anyway. A voluntary note ("Sprecherstimme KI-generiert, Inhalte recherchiert und geprüft von …", "narrator voice AI-generated, content researched and checked by …") still costs little and matches the 85 % demand for labels.
- This is not legal advice. Confirm the details against the Official Journal and the Commission's Code of Practice on marking and labelling.

### Gaps
- The Commission's final Code of Practice or guidelines on Article 50 labelling were not retrieved.
- I did not check whether YouTube has added a specific EU AI Act statement since August 2026.
- I did not check Germany's national enforcement authority (likely the Bundesnetzagentur) or its fines.

## 5. YouTube monetisation: "inauthentic content" (the July 2025 rename) and "reused content"

### Takeaway
On 15 July 2025 YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content". YouTube says this was a clarification, not a new ban. What is not allowed is templated or mass-produced material with little narrative or educational value, and **readings of material you did not create**. Stock footage with an original, sourced, narrated script is acceptable in principle. The risk comes from sameness across uploads and from reading other people's texts.

### Cited Findings
- The YouTube Help page on channel monetisation policies: the 15 July 2025 changelog renames "repetitious" to "inauthentic content" (the section is titled "Generic or Repetitive Content"). It does not allow "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value" or "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production". It does allow the same intro and outro when the bulk of each video differs — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Reused content means repurposing content that is already on YouTube or another online source. Not allowed: compilations with little narrative, and "Content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create". Allowed: footage from others with an added storyline and commentary — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- The policy page does not mention AI voices or stock footage explicitly — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- YouTube's Rene Ritchie called it a "minor update" to long-standing YPP policy. Authentic AI-assisted content stays acceptable, and the policy is applied to whole channels — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-monetization-update-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/)
- YouTube deleted 16 AI channels in July 2026, with the focus on mass production with no original idea — [Samsung Magazine DE](https://samsungmagazine.eu/de/2026/07/29/youtube-nekompromisne-cisti-platformu-47-miliardy-zhlednuti-je-pryc-kvuli-ai-spamu/)

### Inferences
- For a stock-footage plus AI-voice channel, the safeguards are: an original script per video with cited studies (educational value); varied structure, not one template; no reading of articles or Wikipedia pages word for word; some original elements (own diagrams, data, a named editor or vet reviewer). Because YouTube judges whole channels, consistency across all uploads matters.
- Pexels clips are also on other platforms, so a video built *only* from stock clips with weak narration could look like "reused" material. A strong narrative layer is what makes it transformative.

### Gaps
- YouTube publishes no figure for how much AI voice or stock footage triggers a YPP rejection. Evidence for that is anecdotal only.

## 6. Pexels license terms for commercial YouTube use

### Takeaway
All Pexels photos and videos are free to use, also commercially, with no attribution required and with modification allowed. There are limits around identifiable people, implied endorsement, reselling unaltered copies, trademarks, and redistribution on other stock sites.

### Cited Findings
- "All photos and videos on Pexels are free to use." Attribution is not required but is appreciated. Modification is allowed. Listed uses include ads, marketing, websites and social media — [Pexels License](https://www.pexels.com/license/)
- Not allowed: showing identifiable people in a bad light or offensive way; selling unaltered copies (posters, prints, products); implying that people or brands in the imagery endorse something; redistributing or selling the content on other stock or wallpaper platforms; using it as part of a trademark, trade name or service mark — [Pexels License](https://www.pexels.com/license/)
- The license page does not mention YouTube monetisation or AI explicitly — [Pexels License](https://www.pexels.com/license/)

### Inferences
- Monetised YouTube videos built from edited Pexels clips are covered. The practical risks are (a) Content ID claims, because other uploaders sometimes register stock clips, and (b) the reused-content judgement on YouTube's side (see question 5), not the license.
- A Pexels dog clip should not be used for the channel logo or brand mark (the trademark restriction).

### Gaps
- No official Pexels statement on Content ID disputes was retrieved.

## 7. German YouTube viewing habits: TV-screen share, video lengths, pet-content popularity

### Takeaway
YouTube is the most-used video platform in Germany: about 72 % daily reach among people aged 14 and over, per the ARD/ZDF-Medienstudie 2025 as reported, and 21 M+ Germans watch it on a Smart TV each month. Globally, YouTube's TV-screen share of viewing rose to about 34 % by Q4 2025. I found no Germany-specific watch-time-on-TV share and no data on German pet-content length or popularity.

### Cited Findings
- ARD/ZDF-Medienstudie 2025: YouTube leads overall video use, ahead of the ARD/ZDF Mediatheken. One summary gives a 72 % daily reach (14+). These figures come from secondary summaries — [onlinemarketing.de](https://onlinemarketing.de/cases/ard-zdf-medienstudie-2025); [utopia.de](https://utopia.de/utopia-insights/ardzdf-medienstudie-zwischen-podcasts-social-video-und-digitalem-wandel/)
- AGF Plattformstudie 2025-II: 68.8 % of TV reception is now internet-based, mostly via the Smart TV — [AGF PM 21.01.2026](https://www.agf.de/fileadmin/agf/service/Pressemitteilungen/2026/260121_PM_Plattform/260121_PM_AGF_Plattformstudie_2025-II_docx.pdf)
- Google, for YouTube's 20th anniversary: YouTube has 56 M users in Germany, and more than 21 M Germans watch YouTube on a Smart TV every month — [GoogleWatchBlog](https://www.googlewatchblog.de/2025/04/20-jahre-youtube-die-videoplattform-hat-56-millionen-nutzer-in-deutschland-google-nennt-viele-statistiken-u/amp/)
- Cross-market (Digital i): the TV-screen share of YouTube viewing time rose from 28.2 % in Q1 2024 to 33.7 % in Q4 2025. It is not stated that Germany is in the sample — [Digital i](https://www.digital-i.com/news-articles/press-release-youtube-overtakes-netflix-for-average-daily-viewing-time-while-netflix-channel-achieves-widest-reach-of-2025)
- A DACH device split of 60 % mobile, 26 % connected TV, 11 % desktop and 3 % tablet. The method and period are unclear, so treat it as weak — [Bravery Technology](https://braverytechnology.com/de/?p=3089)
- Nearly half of German children watch YouTube on a TV (2026) — [Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/01/29/germany-youtube-vod-dominate-kids-media-habits)

### Inferences
- A meaningful share of viewing (roughly a quarter to a third) happens on TV screens. That favours longer, calm, well-lit videos in real HD footage that are watched in a "lean-back" way, and here Pexels HD or 4K clips are an asset. A synthetic voice is also more exposed on living-room speakers, so voice quality matters.

- No data on typical video lengths for German how-to or pet content.
- No data on how popular pet content is on German YouTube, for example category rankings. The project's own radar (dog-psych-de) is the best available source for this.
